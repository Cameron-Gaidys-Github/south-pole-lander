What is modelled, and what is estimated
  • Point-mass flight in the Moon's central gravity field, with curvature, from a 15 × 100 km orbit. Attitude is rate-limited; throttles lag and cannot go below each engine's floor.
  • Starship HLS brakes on Raptors, then hands over to mid-body landing thrusters near the surface, because one Raptor at minimum throttle would still push a ~250 t lander upward at over 2 m/s². Thruster count and thrust are estimates.
  • Blue Moon MK2 flies three BE-7 hydrogen engines (44 kN, 460 s, 20–100% throttle, published). Its propellant load and geometry are estimates.
  • Guidance is an energy-optimal ZEM/ZEV law re-solved every step, flown through gates like Apollo's P63/P64/P66. If an engine failure leaves no landing solution, it aborts to orbit.
  • Sunlight is traced across the terrain for every vertex, including the Moon's curvature and the Sun's 0.53° disk. No atmosphere: black sky, no haze, dust flies on ballistic arcs.
  • Touchdown is judged on sink rate, drift, attitude, slope across the footpads, boulders under pads and tip-over margin from the centre-of-mass height.
